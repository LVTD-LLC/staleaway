For the editor keeping a small nonprofit's public pages current

You can use Staleaway when you already have someone who can check facts and edit your website, but need a recurring prompt to return to pages. You still make the editorial decisions and publish changes in your own website system.

A program page can outlast its original plan

A visitor reads the hours on a program page and decides when to come. The editor may know how to change that page without knowing whether the hours are still right. Put the program coordinator's current operating schedule beside the public claim before deciding the page needs no changes.

A volunteer page needs an answer from someone running the activity

An opportunity can remain visible after an intake closes. A reminder can bring the page back to your attention, but it cannot tell you whether places remain. Ask the volunteer coordinator about the current role, application route and next intake. Known closures need a direct update, not a wait for the next reminder.

A departing contact leaves more than a biography

When a named staff contact changes, check the places that send visitors to them: a program description, contact page or linked document. Keep the edit and its follow-up with the person responsible for publishing. An extra email recipient in Staleaway is not a replacement editor or a transfer of account access.

Choose the next action before setting a cadence

Use this decision rule for nonprofit website content reviews: correct a known wrong fact now; put a fixed publication date in a calendar or CMS task; use recurring reminders for routine revisits. These are different jobs, even when they concern the same page.

Keep the fact check outside the reminder

For a page that needs another person's input, put the public claim, its fact source and the next action in your own working notes. The examples below are fictional. This is a suggested manual method, not a Staleaway assignment form or approval record.

  • Program page: “Drop-in hours are Tuesday afternoons.” Ask the program coordinator to compare that statement with the current operating schedule. If the hours changed, have the website editor correct the page and check any linked visitor instructions.
  • Volunteer page: “Applications are open.” Ask the volunteer coordinator to confirm that the role is still available. If there is a closing date, assign a dated update in your own calendar rather than relying on a sampled reminder.
  • Event page: “The workshop is at the community hall.” Compare it with the organizer's approved event details. A venue move or cancellation needs a direct edit and a check of other pages linking to the event.
  • Contact page: “Ask our outreach lead.” Confirm who owns that role and whether the published contact route is current. Record who will publish the correction and when you will check it, without adding private contact records to a public page.

After publishing a correction, check the live page and linked instructions before recording the issue resolved. If confirmation is missing, keep a named follow-up owner and date in your notes. Opening a Staleaway review link marks the page reviewed before redirecting to it; it does not prove the fact check or correction happened. How Staleaway supports nonprofit website content reviews

Start with public pages in an XML sitemap

Add the XML sitemap containing the public pages you want to revisit, then inspect the imported list. A sitemap is not a full inventory of drafts, private documents or every page in your CMS. The sitemap setup guide explains how to find the input and reconcile missing pages.

Choose a manageable email batch

Choose daily, weekly or monthly email cadence and the pages per email. Selection samples eligible unreviewed pages; it is not an ordered schedule or a guarantee of when a particular page appears. Use the free content review workload planner to estimate the manual time you can set aside.

Use review links from the owning account

Review links require signing in to the account that owns those page records. Additional email recipients do not get separate reviewer permissions. Ask colleagues for facts through your usual communication channel; do not share account credentials or treat a forwarded review link as a task assignment.

Keep your existing editing tools

Staleaway does not edit your CMS, publish corrections or approve content. It provides the prompt to look again. Keep your own calendar for deadlines and your usual notes for unresolved checks. If you need automatic alerts about page changes or technical conditions, compare content review reminders and website change monitoring before choosing.

Start with a review habit you can maintain

  1. Choose the account owner who will receive reminders and can coordinate website edits.
  2. Find your public XML sitemap, add it to Staleaway and check which pages imported.
  3. Choose your email cadence and pages per email. Reserve time for the human review.
  4. Use a real reminder to open a page while signed in. Confirm facts with the responsible person and record unfinished work separately.

Every feature is currently free. You do not need a paid nonprofit plan. If an existing CMS review queue or calendar already prompts the right person reliably, keeping that workflow may be enough.

Before you choose reminders

Is this a nonprofit website maintenance service?

No. Staleaway sends content review reminders. It does not provide website backups, security updates, uptime monitoring, donation processing, accessibility certification or a managed editing service.

Will every page be checked by our next event?

No. Email cadence does not guarantee review coverage or a per-page deadline. Put an event launch, registration closure or other fixed date in your own calendar or CMS workflow, and check the affected pages directly.

Can several volunteers independently approve pages?

No. Staleaway has no team approval or assignment workflow. Review links are tied to the owning account. Colleagues can confirm facts outside the app; the account owner remains responsible for using its review links.

Should we add private beneficiary or donor information?

No. Use this workflow for public website pages. Keep private records, unpublished documents and credentials in your existing restricted systems, not in a public sitemap or a shared review note.

What this approach is based on

The Nonprofit Support Network's website guidance recommends checking program information, staff details and contact routes, and having coworkers notify the responsible editor. YMCA Website Services' content-audit guidance includes an inventory of unpublished CMS content, a broader task than reviewing a public sitemap.

Staleaway's examples and decision rule are recommendations, not findings from nonprofit customers. Product capabilities were checked against the Staleaway source on October 23, 2026. Reminder delivery is not proof of a completed content review.
